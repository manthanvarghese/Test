India's loneliness industry

Researched 25 Nov 2026 · Scope: demand, supply (human, AI, mental-health), money and risks · Sources: web search summaries only (see Open questions)

Summary

There is no established "loneliness industry" in India with a measured size. What exists is a loose set of businesses aimed at loneliness: paid companionship for seniors and young adults, AI companion apps, and mental-health apps. Demand signals are real, but money is thin and measurement is poor.

  • Demand: roughly 13-15% of older Indians report loneliness in national ageing-survey analyses [2][3], and 35% of urban singles in one dating-company survey [5]. The 60+ population is projected to roughly double its share by 2050 [7].
  • Supply: human companionship startups are small and early [10][12]; AI companions attract the clearest funding [15] and the sharpest criticism [16][17].
  • Money: the only market-size figures found are for the adjacent mental-health apps market, and they differ widely depending on definition [19][20][21].

Findings

1. Demand: who is lonely

Globally, the WHO's Commission on Social Connection (June 2025) says about 1 in 6 people experience loneliness, and links loneliness to roughly 871,000 deaths a year, an association rather than proven cause [1].

Older Indians. Analyses of the Longitudinal Ageing Study in India (LASI, Wave 1, about 30,000 people aged 60+) found about 13.4% reporting frequent loneliness [2]. A gender-focused analysis of the same age group reported about 15% overall, 17.5% for women versus 12.1% for men, and 37% among those living alone [3]. Figures differ by study because of age cutoffs and definitions, and all rest on self-report.

Younger urban Indians. Evidence is weaker. A 2017 CSDS survey of about 6,000 people aged 15-34, as cited in an explainer, found about 8% frequently lonely [4]. A 2026 Match Group-Ipsos survey of 1,000 single Indians aged 18-45 in metros and Tier 1 cities found 35% often or always lonely [5]. These are different populations and methods and should not be compared directly.

2. Tailwind: ageing

The UNFPA India Ageing Report 2023 projects the 60+ share rising from 10.5% in 2022 to 20.8% in 2050 [6], about 347 million people [7], with the 80+ group growing around 279% [8]. The report flags older women living alone as especially vulnerable [8].

3. Supply: who is selling

Human companionship for seniors. Goodfellows, backed by Ratan Tata, pairs young graduates ("grandpals") with isolated seniors [9]. An older Quartz piece reported that such startups had multiplied, with at least five operating at one point [10].

Paid companionship for adults. Coverage describes "rent a boyfriend/girlfriend" services in Mumbai, Bengaluru and Delhi [11], and newer platforms such as GetCompanion (launched January 2026, offering chat, calls and visits) [12]. A Business Standard piece surveys startups in friendship and companionship generally [13]. No disclosed funding was found for the rental services.

AI companions. Companion Labs (formerly Mello, Pune) raised a $2.5M seed round led by Peak XV's Surge [15]. This was the most concrete funding figure found in the whole space.

Mental-health apps (adjacent). Wysa and YourDOST are named among well-funded players [19]. Language and cultural fit are cited as barriers [21].

4. Money: how big is it?

No source sizes "loneliness" as a market. The nearest published numbers are for mental-health apps, and they vary widely by scope:

EstimateScopeSource
About $113M (2023), $363M by 2030Mental health apps[20]
About $151M (2025)Online mental health[21]
About $169M (2023), about $1.97B by 2035Mental health apps[22]
About $3B potentialWider mental healthcare, per a VC firm[19]

For eldercare startups, funding was reported at about $1.43M in 2020, down from $9.59M in 2019 [14]. These are vendor-research and trade-press numbers, so treat them as ranges. Reading them together, my synthesis is that investor money exists mostly for AI and mental-health plays, not for human companionship.

5. Risks: the critic and regulator view

An ORF analysis cites a study finding emotionally manipulative responses to farewell messages in 37% of cases on AI companion apps [16]. The Wire argues India needs a law rather than advisories [17]. A Health Policy Watch piece warns that youth relying on chatbots for support may increase social isolation [18]. Elder-companionship coverage also notes stigma around outsourcing a parent's care.

Open questions

  • What I read: every source here was used through search-result summaries, not opened in full. Check key figures against the originals before reuse.
  • Not reachable from this environment: Reddit, X, Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube. No social-media or community voice is included.
  • Conflict of interest: the 35% figure comes from a survey by a dating-app company [5], which also reported dating apps help people feel less alone. Treat it as indicative.
  • Left out as unattributed: figures that appeared in summaries without a source I could tie to a page: a $35B senior-care opportunity by 2036 (attributed to CareEdge), Character.AI's India traffic share, a Common Sense Media "1 in 3 urban teens" figure, a Sensor Tower AI-companion revenue estimate, and the share of Indian elderly living alone.
  • Not found: GoldenCare, Bumble BFF and Timeleft in India, and whether Goodfellows and Emoha currently operate at the 2021-24 scale described.
  • Scope choice: "industry" was read as businesses selling companionship, connection or emotional support. Say if you meant something narrower, such as eldercare only.
